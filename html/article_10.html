Allergic Disease and Sensitization - 5 Year Follow-Up of an Ethiopian Birth Cohort - A summary

Date: 02/08/26

This study explores if paracetamol use during early childhood increases the risk of developing allergic diseases such as asthma (wheeze), eczema, rhinitis, and allergic sensitization. The researchers conducted a study in Butajira, Ethiopia. The study was based on children from the time they were born until five years of age to examine paracetamol use in relation to allergic reactions.

The study started with 1,006 participants, with 863 children remaining in the study at five years of age. Information other factors that could influence the study was collected when the children were 1, 3, and 5 years old. Tests were carried out to discover common allergens were done at ages 3 and 5. Multivariable logistic regression was used to estimate the independent relationship between early-life paracetamol exposure and the later development of allergic diseases.

The results showed that paracetamol use was common. Children who used paracetamol were more likely to develop wheeze, eczema, rhinitis, and allergic sensitization between the ages of three and five. This is more likely for wheeze and eczema. Furthermore, frequent use of paracetamol was linked with a more chances of allergic diseases at five years.

The researchers admit that, as an observational study, it could not fully establish causation, and unmeasured factors may could have influenced the results.
