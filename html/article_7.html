An Introduction to Knowledge Management - A summary

Date: 18/04/26

1. Introduction

Knowledge is information that is being used and it is what an organization knows about their customers, products, processes, mistakes, and successes.

Knowledge management (KM) is a method to capture knowledge in an organization

Effective knowledge management needs to mix organizational, social, and managerial activities with the right technology.

The two types of knowledge:

  • Tacit:
    • Tacit knowledge is knowledge the individual is not aware of.
    • For example, it is very difficult to explain how to drive a car.
    • Tacit knowledge is hard to obtain.
    • It's hard to manage.
    • With experience and continuous learning, the tacit knowledge becomes explicit if it ends up being documented.
  • Explicit / formal:
    • It is knowledge that is readily available and written down. For example, it is very simple to instruct someone how to calculate the area of a rectangle by telling the formula.
    • Some examples of this form of knowledge are: reports, articles, manuals, patents, pictures, images, video, sound, software etc.
    • Explicit knowledge is the identity, skills and the intellectual assets of an organization independently of its employees.

2. Knowledge Management at TATA Steel

Tata Steel is used as a case study of effective Knowledge management. The Knowledge Management is one of the reasons why Tata Steel is self reliant in technology.

Tata Steel aimed at get knowledge from working groups and agencies.

The KM Portal was deployed to share both forms of knowledge across the entire company.

It is an online knowledge repository that users can submit - knowledge can be added by an individual (codification) or a team or a group of people (personalization) -, search and use the knowledge in it. The portal also provides a virtual forum.

The first two strategies, codification and personalization, enable capture and storage of knowledge, whereas knowledge diffusion takes the benefit of replicating best practices identified in the repository.

3. Secure Knowledge Management

Example of security methods: authentication or passwords, cryptography programs, intrusion detection systems, trust management, privacy control, access control systems, policies and procedures.

Some issues KM can face are: malicious insiders, subversion attacks and wrong policies. The importance of security is exacerbated by the KMS having more sensitive content than raw data stored in databases.

Security has to be incorporated into all parts of the organization.

It is crucial to use various metrics that should focus on the impact of security methods implemented.
